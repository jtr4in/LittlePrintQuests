# Vet kit X-ray scans: Gemini Pro prompts (2026-11-11)

Two sets of full-page X-ray scans, one page per animal. Paste each prompt into Gemini (Pro image model), **landscape 4:3**, the biggest size it offers. Every prompt asks for an anatomically correct skeleton; check the bones against the notes under each animal before keeping a picture, and redo any that get it wrong (extra ribs, a bendy spine, missing kneecaps, bird arms with fingers).

- **Set 1, little kids (can't read yet):** no words at all. Round picture bubbles point to the parts (a heart, lungs, the brain, a bone), so kids can find and point.
- **Set 2, big kids (early readers):** the same scan with the real bone names in short labels, plus one "Did you know?" fact.

Save them as `xray-little-<animal>.png` and `xray-big-<animal>.png` and send them over; I'll fit them into the kit (little set in the little kids version, labelled set in the big kids version).

---

## The shared style (already inside every prompt below)

> A realistic veterinary X-ray (radiograph) printed as a full-page poster for children ages 3 to 8. Glowing white and pale blue bones on a deep navy-black film, with the soft grey see-through outline of the animal's body and fur around the skeleton, like a real X-ray on a light box. The skeleton must be anatomically correct for this species: the right number and shape of bones, joints in the right places, nothing invented, nothing missing. Side view, the whole animal fits on the film with a wide margin. A rounded teal frame around the film, a clean white page border. No faces or smiles on any objects. No cast shadows.

---

**Tips:** Gemini Pro spells better than Flash, but still check every label letter by letter. If an animal comes out with a cartoon face on the skeleton, add "the skull is a real bone skull, not a cartoon face". If it draws extra legs or a curly spine, say "exactly four legs, straight natural spine".
