Superhero scavenger hunt for kids

A rhyming superhero treasure hunt for ages 4–8. Read the story, hide the clues around the house and let the rookie heroes follow the trail to the secret clue. Here are free clues to start with, and a printable version with your child’s name and your own hiding spots.

Make a personalized Superhero hunt

Start with the story

Read this out loud before the first clue. It turns “go find some cards” into a mission.

Classified Transmission

Attention, Rookie Heroes!

This is the Chief of the Hero Alliance. This message is TOP SECRET, so listen closely. Every great hero must pass the Secret HQ Trial before they can earn their official hero cape.

I have hidden eight encrypted clues around this base. Each clue is a rhyming riddle. Crack the code, use your super-speed, and you’ll find the next one.

Remember the hero rules: work as a team, help your friends, and never give up. Only the bravest rookies will make it all the way to the end of the trial!

Your first clue is attached. The Alliance is counting on you!

Stay super,
The Chief of the Hero Alliance

5 free rhyming clues

Write each riddle on a card and hide it at the answer to the clue before it. The first clue goes in the kids’ hands after the story.

  1. Clue 1

    Where do you sleep when the moon’s shining bright?
    Look under the pillow where you snuggle at night!

    Answer: Bed. Hide it: Under the pillow.

  2. Clue 2

    Sneakers and boots standing all in a row,
    Check where the shoes rest when they’re done on the go!

    Answer: Shoe Rack. Hide it: Inside a shoe on the shoe rack.

  3. Clue 3

    Where do the cartoons and movies all play?
    Check by the TV, but don’t watch today!

    Answer: TV. Hide it: Under the TV remote.

  4. Clue 4

    It says “Welcome!” and wipes muddy feet,
    Look under the doormat, now isn’t that neat?

    Answer: Doormat. Hide it: Under the doormat.

  5. Clue 5

    Where can you build a big castle of sand?
    Check by the sandbox, it’s ever so grand!

    Answer: Sandbox. Hide it: In a zip bag on the sandbox edge.

Between clues, read a superhero rhyme to keep the story going:

Attention, rookies! Your training starts now,
Use your super-smart brains and I’ll show you how!

Great work, hero! Your powers are strong,
Now zoom to the next spot, it won’t take you long!

One final mission, then capes for the crew!
Your hero reward is waiting for you!

Make it right for their age

  • Ages 4 to 5: add a picture of the hiding spot to every card so pre-readers can solve it, and keep the trail to 6 spots.
  • Ages 6 and up: let the kids read the riddles themselves, use 8 to 10 spots and add a silly challenge at each stop.
  • A big group: give each child a turn to read a clue or find a card so everyone gets a win.

Questions

What age is a superhero scavenger hunt for?

This hunt is written for ages 4–8. Younger kids follow the picture on each card while a grown-up reads the rhyme; older kids can read the clues on their own.

How many clues should a scavenger hunt have?

Six to ten clues is the sweet spot for ages 3 to 8. It takes about 15 to 25 minutes, long enough to feel like a quest and short enough that nobody loses interest.

Can I do this hunt in a small apartment?

Yes. Pick spots in every room and zig-zag between them. The apartment trail below uses eight spots every apartment has.
