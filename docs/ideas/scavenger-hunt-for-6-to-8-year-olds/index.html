Scavenger hunt for 6, 7 and 8 year olds

Early readers want clues they can solve without help, and something to prove at every stop. Here’s an eight-clue trail with trickier spots, challenges to add between clues and codes for the kids who finish too fast.

Make a big-kid hunt

8 free rhyming clues

Let them read each riddle on their own. These spots are a little harder to guess than the couch and the bed.

  1. Clue 1

    Tick-tock, tick-tock, what time can it be?
    Check by the clock, and then you will see!

    Answer: Clock. Hide it: Below a wall clock.

  2. Clue 2

    It sloshes and splashes and swishes around,
    It washes your clothes with a rumbly sound!

    Answer: Washing Machine. Hide it: On top of the washing machine.

  3. Clue 3

    Who’s that smiling right back at you?
    Check by the mirror, you’ll see something new!

    Answer: Mirror. Hide it: Taped to the corner of a mirror.

  4. Clue 4

    Where are the crackers and cereal kept?
    Peek in the pantry where snacks have all slept!

    Answer: Pantry. Hide it: Next to the cereal in the pantry.

  5. Clue 5

    It says “Welcome!” and wipes muddy feet,
    Look under the doormat, now isn’t that neat?

    Answer: Doormat. Hide it: Under the doormat.

  6. Clue 6

    Where are the pencils, the paper, and pens?
    Check by the desk, and then count to ten!

    Answer: Desk. Hide it: Under the keyboard or a notebook.

  7. Clue 7

    Where do the jackets hang out on a hook?
    Open the closet and take a quick look!

    Answer: Coat Closet. Hide it: In a coat pocket inside the closet.

  8. Clue 8

    Head to the spot where wet clothes become dry,
    It spins and it tumbles, so give it a try!

    Answer: Dryer. Hide it: Inside the dryer drum (door open).

Add a challenge at every stop

Write a challenge on the back of each card. They have to do it before they can read the next clue:

  • Hop on one foot ten times.
  • Spell your name backwards.
  • Find three things in this room that start with B.
  • Do your best dinosaur roar (or pirate laugh, or superhero pose).
  • Count backwards from 20 as fast as you can.

Add a secret code

  • Number code: A is 1, B is 2. Write the hiding spot as numbers, like 2-5-4 for BED.
  • Mirror writing: write the clue backwards so they need a mirror to read it.
  • Scrambled word: write the answer with its letters mixed up, like GDRIEF for FRIDGE.
  • Puzzle finale: put one puzzle piece with each clue; the finished puzzle shows where the treasure is.

Hand them the job

Kids this age love running a hunt as much as playing one. Once they’ve done yours, let them write and hide clues for a younger sibling or for you. It’s writing practice they’ll actually want to do.

Questions

How many clues for a 7 year old’s scavenger hunt?

Eight to ten clues, with a challenge at some of the stops, takes about 20 to 30 minutes.

How do you make a scavenger hunt harder?

Use spots that are harder to guess, add a code they have to crack to read each clue, and add a challenge at each stop.

Can older and younger kids do the same hunt?

Yes. Pair a reader with a pre-reader: the big kid reads the clue, the little one finds it.
