Valentine’s scavenger hunt for kids

A rhyming valentine’s treasure hunt for ages 3–7. Read the story, hide the clues around the house and let the valentine helpers follow the trail to the paper heart. Here are free clues to start with, and a printable version with your child’s name and your own hiding spots.

Make a personalized Valentine’s hunt

Start with the story

Read this out loud before the first clue. It turns “go find some cards” into a mission.

A Letter from Pip

Hello, Valentine Helpers!

It’s me, Pip the Love Bug, Cupid’s littlest helper! Every Valentine’s Day I fly around delivering friendship notes, but this morning my mailbag snagged on a rosebush. Pop! The valentines fluttered out and floated all over this place.

I followed the trail of paper hearts and tucked eight Love Note cards along the way. Each one has a rhyming riddle that tells you where to look next.

Valentine Helpers are kind, gentle and great at sharing. Work together, take turns, and shout “Buzz-buzz, hooray!” every time you find a card. Follow the trail to the very end, where a sweet surprise is waiting!

Your first Love Note card is attached. Happy Valentine’s Day, helpers!

Hugs and buzzes,
Pip the Love Bug, Cupid’s Helper

5 free rhyming clues

Write each riddle on a card and hide it at the answer to the clue before it. The first clue goes in the kids’ hands after the story.

  1. Clue 1

    Tick-tock, tick-tock, what time can it be?
    Check by the clock, and then you will see!

    Answer: Clock. Hide it: Below a wall clock.

  2. Clue 2

    It’s tall and it’s leafy with branches up high,
    Check by the big tree that reaches the sky!

    Answer: Big Tree. Hide it: At the base of the tree trunk.

  3. Clue 3

    Where do your toys go when playtime is done?
    Look in the place where you keep all the fun!

    Answer: Toy Box. Hide it: On top of the toys in the toy box.

  4. Clue 4

    Where are the crackers and cereal kept?
    Peek in the pantry where snacks have all slept!

    Answer: Pantry. Hide it: Next to the cereal in the pantry.

  5. Clue 5

    Where are the pencils, the paper, and pens?
    Check by the desk, and then count to ten!

    Answer: Desk. Hide it: Under the keyboard or a notebook.

Between clues, read a valentine’s rhyme to keep the story going:

Uh-oh, Valentine Helpers, my mailbag went POP!
The love notes flew out and they just wouldn’t stop!

A love note, hooray! It’s rosy and red,
Now flutter along to the next spot ahead!

One note is left, and it’s the biggest of all,
Find it and share it with friends big and small!

Make it right for their age

  • Ages 3 to 5: add a picture of the hiding spot to every card so pre-readers can solve it, and keep the trail to 6 spots.
  • Ages 6 and up: let the kids read the riddles themselves, use 8 to 10 spots and add a silly challenge at each stop.
  • A big group: give each child a turn to read a clue or find a card so everyone gets a win.

Questions

What age is a valentine’s scavenger hunt for?

This hunt is written for ages 3–7. Younger kids follow the picture on each card while a grown-up reads the rhyme; older kids can read the clues on their own.

How many clues should a scavenger hunt have?

Six to ten clues is the sweet spot for ages 3 to 8. It takes about 15 to 25 minutes, long enough to feel like a quest and short enough that nobody loses interest.

Can I do this hunt in a small apartment?

Yes. Pick spots in every room and zig-zag between them. The apartment trail below uses eight spots every apartment has.
