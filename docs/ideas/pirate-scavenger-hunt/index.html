Pirate scavenger hunt for kids

A rhyming pirate treasure hunt for ages 3–7. Read the story, hide the clues around the house and let the pirate crew follow the trail to the map piece. Here are free clues to start with, and a printable version with your child’s name and your own hiding spots.

Make a personalized Pirate hunt

Start with the story

Read this out loud before the first clue. It turns “go find some cards” into a mission.

A Letter from the Captain

Ahoy, Pirate Crew!

Captain Barnaby here, the most famous pirate on the seven seas. I buried my treasure chest somewhere close by, but that silly parrot of mine got into my ledger and scrambled the map into rhymes!

Now there are eight pieces of the map hidden around this ship. Each piece has a riddle that points to where the next one is hiding.

A good pirate crew sticks together, so take turns, share the clues, and give a big “ARRR!” every time you find one. Follow the map to the very end and the treasure is yours to keep!

Here be the first piece of the map. Fair winds, mateys!

Yer Captain,
Captain Barnaby

5 free rhyming clues

Write each riddle on a card and hide it at the answer to the clue before it. The first clue goes in the kids’ hands after the story.

  1. Clue 1

    Where do the stories sit snug in a row?
    Peek by the books, where the readers all go!

    Answer: Bookshelf. Hide it: Tucked between two books.

  2. Clue 2

    It sloshes and splashes and swishes around,
    It washes your clothes with a rumbly sound!

    Answer: Washing Machine. Hide it: On top of the washing machine.

  3. Clue 3

    Where does your furry friend curl up to snore?
    Check the pet’s bed, then you’ll find something more!

    Answer: Pet Bed. Hide it: Tucked in the pet’s bed.

  4. Clue 4

    Where do your teddies and stuffies all sleep?
    Check with your stuffies, they’re piled in a heap!

    Answer: Stuffed Animals. Hide it: Held by a teddy bear.

  5. Clue 5

    Where do we snuggle for movies and naps?
    Check under the cushions, it’s there, perhaps!

    Answer: Couch. Hide it: Under a couch cushion.

Between clues, read a pirate rhyme to keep the story going:

Ahoy, little mateys! Now gather around,
Captain Barnaby’s treasure is waiting to be found!

Yo ho ho, matey, you’re sharp as a hook!
The parrot squawks, “Here’s the next place to look!”

Batten the hatches, the end is in sight,
Captain Barnaby’s treasure is hidden just right!

Make it right for their age

  • Ages 3 to 5: add a picture of the hiding spot to every card so pre-readers can solve it, and keep the trail to 6 spots.
  • Ages 6 and up: let the kids read the riddles themselves, use 8 to 10 spots and add a silly challenge at each stop.
  • A big group: give each child a turn to read a clue or find a card so everyone gets a win.

Questions

What age is a pirate scavenger hunt for?

This hunt is written for ages 3–7. Younger kids follow the picture on each card while a grown-up reads the rhyme; older kids can read the clues on their own.

How many clues should a scavenger hunt have?

Six to ten clues is the sweet spot for ages 3 to 8. It takes about 15 to 25 minutes, long enough to feel like a quest and short enough that nobody loses interest.

Can I do this hunt in a small apartment?

Yes. Pick spots in every room and zig-zag between them. The apartment trail below uses eight spots every apartment has.
