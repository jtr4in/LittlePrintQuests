Police Station pretend play for kids

Everything your child needs to run a friendly pretend police station. Print on regular Letter paper, cut along the dashed lines, and line up some stuffed animals to be visitors. Here’s how to set it up, games to play and a printable kit with your child’s name on every page.

Make a personalized Police Station kit

How to set it up

  1. Tape the station sign and Open sign by the “front door”.
  2. Set up the front desk with a bell and the desk labels.
  3. Make a Lost and Found box and hide a few toys.
  4. Tape the Stop and Crosswalk signs along a hallway “road”.
  5. Pin on the badges and start the patrol!

Build it from a box

Make a Police Car from a cardboard box and the pieces in your kit. Grown-ups do the cutting, kids paint, glue and play.

Games to play

  • 🔍 Lost and found

    A visitor reports a lost toy. Fill in a report and follow the clues.

  • 🚓 On patrol

    Drive around the Town Patrol Map and help at every stop.

  • 🅿️ Parking tickets

    Give toy cars silly Parking Tickets, then pay at the front desk.

  • 🚸 Road safety

    Practise How to Cross the Street with the Stop sign.

Who’s who

Hand out the jobs so everyone gets a turn, grown-ups and stuffed animals included:

  • Police Officer
  • Front Desk Helper
  • Traffic Helper
  • Detective
  • Radio Dispatcher
  • Bike Patrol
  • Lost and Found Helper

For toddlers and preschoolers

Little ones love pretend play before they can read. Give them the job with the most doing, like police officer, keep the money to a few big coins and let them circle pictures on the order forms. Five to ten minutes of play is a win at this age.

The printable Police Station kit has your child’s name on the signs, badges and certificate, plus play money, order forms, props and a grown-up guide full of ideas. Pick the little kids version (ages 3 to 5) or the big kids version (6 to 8), and add the activity pack of wipe-and-play pages if you like. It arrives by email in minutes.

Questions

What age is police station pretend play for?

Ages 3 to 8. The little kids version (ages 3 to 5) has big pictures, thick cut lines and order forms they fill in by circling, so no reading or writing is needed. Ages 5 and up get the writing version.

Do I need anything special to print it?

Regular Letter paper works for everything. Cardstock makes the money, menus and props last longer, and sheet protectors turn the activity pages into wipe-clean pages.

How do I set up a pretend police station at home?

Tape the station sign and Open sign by the “front door”. Set up the front desk with a bell and the desk labels. Make a Lost and Found box and hide a few toys. Tape the Stop and Crosswalk signs along a hallway “road”. Pin on the badges and start the patrol!
