Pet Vet scavenger hunt for kids

A rhyming pet vet treasure hunt for ages 3–7. Read the story, hide the clues around the house and let the junior vets follow the trail to the lost pet. Here are free clues to start with, and a printable version with your child’s name and your own hiding spots.

Make a personalized Pet Vet hunt

Start with the story

Read this out loud before the first clue. It turns “go find some cards” into a mission.

Clinic Emergency

Calling all Junior Vets!

This is Dr. Whiskers at the Pet Rescue Clinic. This morning someone left the clinic gate open, and all of our patients wandered off! The puppies, kittens, bunnies and even the hamster are hiding somewhere nearby.

Every pet left a trail of paw prints, and I have tucked eight Paw Print cards along the way. Each one has a rhyming riddle that tells you where to search next.

Vets are gentle, kind and great at working together. Use quiet feet, check every corner, and give a happy bark every time you find a card. Follow the trail to the very end, where a big surprise is waiting!

Your first Paw Print card is attached. Grab your stethoscopes, vets!

Wags and purrs,
Dr. Whiskers, Head Vet

5 free rhyming clues

Write each riddle on a card and hide it at the answer to the clue before it. The first clue goes in the kids’ hands after the story.

  1. Clue 1

    Where do the cartoons and movies all play?
    Check by the TV, but don’t watch today!

    Answer: TV. Hide it: Under the TV remote.

  2. Clue 2

    It says “Welcome!” and wipes muddy feet,
    Look under the doormat, now isn’t that neat?

    Answer: Doormat. Hide it: Under the doormat.

  3. Clue 3

    Where can you build a big castle of sand?
    Check by the sandbox, it’s ever so grand!

    Answer: Sandbox. Hide it: In a zip bag on the sandbox edge.

  4. Clue 4

    You open it wide when a friend comes to play,
    It says “hello” and “goodbye” every day!

    Answer: Front Door. Hide it: Taped to the inside of the front door.

  5. Clue 5

    Head to the spot where wet clothes become dry,
    It spins and it tumbles, so give it a try!

    Answer: Dryer. Hide it: Inside the dryer drum (door open).

Between clues, read a pet vet rhyme to keep the story going:

Oh no, Junior Vets, the clinic gate’s wide!
The pets wandered off, so let’s look far and wide!

Woof! A paw print! You found a clue,
A puppy is waiting somewhere for you!

One pet is left, and it’s waiting for you,
Find it and the whole clinic will cheer, woo-hoo!

Make it right for their age

  • Ages 3 to 5: add a picture of the hiding spot to every card so pre-readers can solve it, and keep the trail to 6 spots.
  • Ages 6 and up: let the kids read the riddles themselves, use 8 to 10 spots and add a silly challenge at each stop.
  • A big group: give each child a turn to read a clue or find a card so everyone gets a win.

Questions

What age is a pet vet scavenger hunt for?

This hunt is written for ages 3–7. Younger kids follow the picture on each card while a grown-up reads the rhyme; older kids can read the clues on their own.

How many clues should a scavenger hunt have?

Six to ten clues is the sweet spot for ages 3 to 8. It takes about 15 to 25 minutes, long enough to feel like a quest and short enough that nobody loses interest.

Can I do this hunt in a small apartment?

Yes. Pick spots in every room and zig-zag between them. The apartment trail below uses eight spots every apartment has.
